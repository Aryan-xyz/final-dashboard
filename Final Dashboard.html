Cross-Topic Physiological Pathways

🧠
Master Axis
Hypothalamus → Pituitary Gland

GnRH, TRH, CRH, GHRH & Somatostatin reach the anterior pituitary via portal circulatory system, while posterior pituitary is under direct neural control.

🩸
Homeostasis
Pancreas α-cells (Glucagon) ↔ Pancreas β-cells (Insulin)

Antagonistic dual regulation: Glucagon induces glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis (hyperglycemic), while Insulin promotes glucose uptake & glycogenesis (hypoglycemic).

🦴
Calcium Balance
Parathyroid (PTH) ↔ Thyroid (TCT)

PTH increases blood Ca²⁺ levels by bone resorption & renal reabsorption (hypercalcemic), while Thyrocalcitonin (TCT) lowers blood Ca²⁺ levels.

⚡
Emergency Axis
Adrenal Medulla → Fight or Flight

Epinephrine & Norepinephrine increase alertness, heart rate, piloerection, sweating, and glycogen breakdown during acute physical or environmental stress.

💧
Fluid & Pressure
ADH (Vasopressin) ↔ ANF (Heart Atria)

ADH reduces water loss in urine by kidney distal tubule reabsorption. ANF secreted by heart atria causes vasodilation to lower elevated blood pressure.

🧪
Non-Endocrine Tissue
GI Tract, Kidney & Heart → Diffused Hormones

Gastrin, Secretin, CCK & GIP regulate digestive juices; Kidney JGA produces Erythropoietin for RBC formation; Heart produces ANF.

Real World Medical Applications

Impact: High

Diabetes & Insulin Therapy

Prolonged hyperglycemia leads to Diabetes Mellitus with glucosuria and ketone bodies. Exogenous insulin therapy restores glucose homeostasis.

Impact: Essential

Thyroid Disorders & Growth

Iodinated salt prevents Goitre. Maternal hypothyroidism causes Cretinism in babies. Exophthalmic goitre (Graves' disease) increases BMR abnormally.

Impact: Emerging

Synthetic Hormone Therapeutics

Recombinant growth hormone treats pituitary dwarfism; synthetic erythropoietin treats anemia in kidney failure; corticosteroids control autoimmune inflammation.

🔗

Seamless Biological Control

Neural control is fast and point-to-point; endocrine regulation is continuous and chemical. Together they coordinate every cell in your body.

6 Big Questions

❓
What
are Hormones?
▼

Hormones are non-nutrient chemicals which act as intercellular messengers and are produced in trace amounts by ductless endocrine glands and diffused tissues.

⭐ Classical Definition: Chemical produced by endocrine glands, released into blood, and transported to distantly located target organs.
💡
Why
do we need Endocrine Coordination?
▼

Nerve fibres do not innervate all cells of the body. Continuous regulation of cellular functions like metabolism, growth, electrolyte balance, and reproduction requires chemical coordination.

⭐ Key Concept: The neural system and endocrine system jointly coordinate and regulate all physiological functions.
👤
Who
controls the Endocrine Cascade?
▼

The Hypothalamus (basal part of diencephalon) contains neurosecretory nuclei producing releasing & inhibiting hormones that command the Pituitary gland.

⭐ Master Gland: The pituitary resides in sella tursica and regulates thyroid, adrenal cortex, and gonads.
📍
Where
are glands located?
▼

Organised endocrine glands: Hypothalamus, Pituitary, Pineal (brain); Thyroid/Parathyroid (neck); Thymus (chest); Adrenal & Pancreas (abdomen); Gonads. Non-endocrine organs: Heart, Kidney, GI tract.

⭐ Diagram Alert: NCERT Figure 19.1 shows all 10 major gland locations in the human body.
⏰
When
are hormones secreted?
▼

Secretion responds to physiological cues: Melatonin during 24-hr diurnal sleep-wake cycle; Catecholamines during acute emergency stress; Insulin/Glucagon during blood glucose fluctuations.

⭐ Emergency Hormones: Adrenaline & Noradrenaline from adrenal medulla trigger fight-or-flight responses.
🔍
How
do hormones work at cellular level?
▼

Hormones bind specific receptors. Peptide hormones bind membrane receptors generating 2nd messengers (cAMP, IP3, Ca²⁺). Steroids & Thyroid hormones bind nuclear receptors regulating gene expression.

⭐ 4 Chemical Groups: 1. Peptides, 2. Steroids, 3. Iodothyronines, 4. Amino-acid derivatives.

🧠 Hypothalamus & Pituitary

• GnRH stimulates, Somatostatin inhibits GH. • Pituitary in sella tursica attached by stalk. • Anterior (pars distalis): GH, PRL, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH. • Pars intermedia: MSH (melanocytes). • Posterior (pars nervosa): Oxytocin & ADH (synthesised by hypothalamus).

🦋 Thyroid & Parathyroid

• T3 & T4 regulate BMR, RBC formation, nutrients. • Goitre (iodine deficiency), Cretinism (maternal hypo). • Graves' disease: hyperthyroidism, protruding eyes, high BMR. • TCT (Thyrocalcitonin) lowers blood Ca²⁺. • PTH (Parathyroid) increases blood Ca²⁺ (hypercalcemic).

⚡ Adrenal Gland

• Medulla: Epinephrine & Norepinephrine (catecholamines). • Emergency hormones for Fight or Flight. • Cortex layers: Zona reticularis (inner), fasciculata (mid), glomerulosa (outer). • Glucocorticoid: Cortisol (anti-inflammatory). • Mineralocorticoid: Aldosterone (Na+ & water retention). • Addison's disease: cortex underproduction.

🩸 Pancreas & Glucose

• Islets of Langerhans (1-2 million cells, 1-2% tissue). • α-cells: Glucagon (glycogenolysis & gluconeogenesis → hyperglycemic). • β-cells: Insulin (glucose uptake & glycogenesis → hypoglycemic). • Diabetes Mellitus: prolonged hyperglycemia, glucosuria, ketone bodies.

🧪 Non-Endocrine Organs

• Heart atrium: ANF (lowers blood pressure via vasodilation). • Kidney JGA: Erythropoietin (stimulates erythropoiesis). • GI Tract: Gastrin (HCl/pepsinogen), Secretin (bicarbonate), CCK (pancreatic enzymes/bile), GIP (inhibits gastric secretion).

⚠️ Top NEET Traps

• Diabetes Insipidus (ADH deficiency) ≠ Diabetes Mellitus (Insulin deficiency). • Oxytocin & ADH are synthesised in Hypothalamus, stored in Posterior Pituitary. • PTH is hypercalcemic; TCT is hypocalcemic. • Catecholamines increase glycogen breakdown (hyperglycemic effect).
